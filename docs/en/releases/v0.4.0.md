# v0.4.0 — Baseline Busan IMD analysis model

## Analysis model

- Validated distributions, outliers, correlations, and spatial autocorrelation across the 2025 candidate indicators for all 206 Busan administrative dongs.
- Normalized nine conditional indicators into six relative-deprivation domain scores on a 0–100 scale.
- Renormalized the published English IMD weights across the six scored domains to calculate B-IMD composite scores, ranks, and deciles.
- Compared nine sensitivity scenarios covering baseline and equal weights, observed missing-value imputation, and systematic domain unavailability.

## Key results

- All 206 dongs receive all six domain scores and a composite score; actual domain-score missingness is zero.
- Composite scores range from `20.956227` to `82.821838`, with a median of `51.048498`.
- Equal and baseline weights have a Spearman rank correlation of `0.935706`, retaining 18 of the 21 baseline top-decile dongs.
- Omitting income or employment creates the largest rank shifts, so interpretation must disclose the limitations of both proxies.

## Reproducibility and collaboration

- Extended the network-free processed-data rebuild through EDA, domain scoring, the composite index, and sensitivity analysis.
- Added bilingual methodology, executable CSV contracts, and checksum reports.
- Added Slack DM completion notices that verify both GitHub Issue and Linear completion after a PR merge.

## Limitations

- This is an experimental open-data model for relative comparison within Busan, not official statistics or a statutory allocation rule.
- Safety remains held because no direct incident indicator is available, and conditional proxies including income and employment retain material uncertainty.
- Domain contributions, priority-area explanations, and policy linkage follow in the M4 work.
