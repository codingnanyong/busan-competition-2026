# v0.6.0 — Administrative-dong living-condition diagnosis and policy visualization

## Analysis scope

- Evaluated whether deprivation patterns across Busan's 206 administrative dongs form stable,
  distinct clusters.
- Combined multidimensional deprivation with ambient-air exposure to identify environmental
  double-burden areas.
- Built a matrix that connects local vulnerability drivers to policy priorities and concrete
  action examples.
- Added an interactive administrative-dong map with three major categories, eight child
  categories, and 13 assessment indicators in a selectable tree.

## Visualization and policy interpretation

- Users can switch the mapped distribution between major and child category scores.
- Selecting a dong reveals the assessment indicators, data evidence, estimation reasons, and
  conditional policy examples behind its score.
- Each major-category result combines its child-category assessments using the displayed shares.
- Refined the title and guidance around a living-condition diagnosis and renamed the ambiguous
  settlement category to `Living infrastructure and housing`.

## Data confidence

- Distinguishes direct observations from estimated, proxy, and interpolated inputs, with a
  row-level reason for every estimated value.
- Documents calibration conditions and limitations so rapidly developing areas such as Myeongji
  are not presented as educationally vulnerable without appropriate context.
- Results are relative screening evidence for exploring policy candidates, not official statistics
  or a statutory funding rule.
- Decisions based on estimated results still require current administrative data and field review.

## Reproducibility and quality

- Extended the network-free rebuild through clustering, environmental overlay, policy matrix, and
  visualization stages.
- Publishes result CSV files, the interactive HTML map, one-page PDF, PNG, and SVG outputs, and
  calculation manifests.
- Passed Ruff, Pyright, cspell, and all 129 tests.
